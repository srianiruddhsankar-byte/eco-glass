    Test conditions

    what does this control?
    0 m ≈ surface, 6000 m ≈ near the deepest transoceanic cable routes. Higher depth means more hydrostatic pressure squeezing the cable, tested against the copper/aluminum tube's crush threshold (rule C3). Same 0–6000 m scale as the Fiber lab, so runs are comparable across both.
    what does this control?
    0 = no external mechanical contact. 100 = the top of the range this simulator uses for a direct anchor drag or trawl-net snag — a sudden, one-time force rather than gradual wear. It's tested against the steel wire armor's tensile capacity (rule C1), the one hard cutoff in this simulator: exceed it and the cable is severed regardless of any other layer.
    what does this control?
    Represents ongoing wear from current movement, sediment, and marine growth against the outer jacket over time — gradual, not a one-time impact like anchor force. Tested against the jacket's abrasion capacity (rule C4); failing this means slow wear-through, not an instant break.
